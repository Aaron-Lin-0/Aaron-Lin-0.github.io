## Objective

The assignment calls for a portable 2.5-DOF system that moves an end effector through at least a 2.5 × 2.5 in work area. It must fit in a backpack or be assembled by one person in less than ten minutes. Our team is developing a joystick-controlled fishing game. The planned mechanism uses two X-direction rails, one Y rail, and a constrained Z motion for 2.5 degrees of freedom. The pictured pen-plotter rig was a preliminary motion study, rather than the final game mechanism.

## Fishing mechanism test

Our team tested the fishing mechanism shown below, which lifts and releases the 3D-printed game pieces from the board. This recording documents the fishing interaction only; it does not show Cartesian motion. We separately tested the current X-Y system and confirmed that it works, but we do not yet have video of that run.

## Earlier motion prototype

We first built one lead-screw stage to check the motor, coupler, bearing support, and carriage travel together. The next prototype crossed two stages and added a motorized pen lift. Foam-board brackets and temporary supports kept the build quick enough to expose motion and control problems before we committed to the final structure.

## Current status

I own the CAD and mechanical-layout work for the fishing mechanism; a teammate is developing the joystick-control code. The team has tested the current X-Y movement and fishing mechanism. The Z component is now being made for the upcoming final demonstration.

The preliminary rig demonstrated coordinated X-Y motion and pen actuation, with rounded corners and line waviness visible in the traces. Positioning accuracy and repeatability remain unmeasured. The prototype tests are team work, and the project remains in progress until the final demonstration.
