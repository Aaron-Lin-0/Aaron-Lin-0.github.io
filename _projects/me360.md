## Objective

The assignment calls for a portable 2.5-DOF system that moves an end effector through at least a 2.5 × 2.5 in work area. It must fit in a backpack or be assembled by one person in less than ten minutes. Our team is developing a joystick-controlled fishing game. The planned mechanism uses two X-direction rails, one Y rail, and a constrained Z motion for 2.5 degrees of freedom. The pictured pen-plotter rig was a preliminary motion study, rather than the final game mechanism.

## Prototype

We first built one lead-screw stage to check the motor, coupler, bearing support, and carriage travel together. The next prototype crossed two stages and added a motorized pen lift. Foam-board brackets and temporary supports kept the build quick enough to expose motion and control problems before we committed to the final structure.

## Motion tests

Our team used G-code to command the early prototype through square and circular paths before the fishing-game build. These silent recordings document coordinated X-Y motion and pen actuation; they do not establish positioning accuracy or repeatability.

## Current design direction

I own the CAD and mechanical-layout work for the fishing mechanism; a teammate is developing the joystick-control code. Current work is resolving carriage constraint, usable travel, packaging, and assembly before the final build. Joystick control and the final constrained-Z mechanism remain in development.

The preliminary rig demonstrated coordinated X-Y motion and pen actuation, with rounded corners and line waviness visible in the traces. Positioning accuracy and repeatability remain unmeasured. The preliminary prototype and path-test evidence are team work; they are not a completed fishing-game demonstration.
