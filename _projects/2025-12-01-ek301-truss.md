## Objective

Our three-person team designed an acrylic truss, analyzed its member forces and buckling limits, built it, and tested it under load. We used the analytical model to compare geometries before committing material to the final structure.

## Analysis

We represented the truss with a connection matrix and solved the joint-equilibrium equations in MATLAB. Compression members were checked against an experimentally fitted buckling relation, while tension members were checked against the material limit used in the course model.

The preliminary report compared two designs. The selected design had a predicted maximum load of **44.93 ± 1.58 oz**, an estimated cost of **$190**, and a theoretical load-to-cost ratio of **0.2368 oz/$**. A later final-model plot predicted **46.59 oz** and identified member 5 as critical.

## Build and test

We built and load-tested the final truss. It met the course's **16 oz minimum load requirement**, but we did not retain a photograph or measured test record. The modeled values above are documented predictions; the physical result is limited to my recollection that the structure passed the minimum.

The pin-jointed model assumed ideal geometry and connections. It did not represent joint alignment, adhesive quality, or construction variation, all of which could reduce the capacity of the built truss.

## My contribution

I worked as one of three team members on the analysis, report, construction, and test. The surviving documentation records the work as a team submission rather than assigning each calculation or fabrication step to one person.
