## Scope

I recreated mechanical parts and assemblies in SolidWorks from instructor-provided engineering drawings. These exercises demonstrate CAD fluency; they are not original product designs.

The course work included:

- parametric part models built from dimensioned drawings;
- assemblies with standard fasteners and purchased components;
- section and exploded views;
- drawing sheets with dimensions and bills of materials; and
- basic motion studies for mechanisms and assemblies.

## Modeling workflow

Each exercise began with a supplied drawing rather than an existing CAD file. I translated the views and dimensions into a feature order, constrained the base sketches, and built repeated or symmetric geometry with patterns and mirrors where the drawing supported them. The final models had to agree with the drawing while remaining readable in the feature tree.

For assemblies, I inserted standard hardware and applied mates that reproduced the intended motion or fixed relationship. Section and exploded views then served different checks: a section exposed the internal interfaces, while an exploded view showed how parts and fasteners fit together without hiding them inside the housing.

## Ball valve assembly

The ball valve exercise combined a housing, ball, shaft, covers, handle, reducers, and fasteners. I assembled the supplied geometry, checked the internal arrangement with section views, and created exploded and motion views.

The section view made the stack of the ball, shaft, housing, covers, and fasteners legible in one frame. The exterior view then confirmed the handle position and the relationship between the two reducers and the main body. Together, the views document both the assembled form and the internal arrangement.

## Other exercises

Other classwork covered a wheel assembly and bill of materials, individual machined parts, a universal joint, and linkage assemblies. Each exercise started with a supplied drawing and ended with a constrained model, assembly, or production drawing.

The wheel exercise added purchased-component callouts and a bill of materials. The universal-joint and linkage exercises focused more on mate selection and motion, where an over-constrained assembly can appear complete but fail when a component is driven. These were modeling and documentation exercises, so I do not present the geometry as original design work.
